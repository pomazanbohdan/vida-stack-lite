# Assign the prototype roles

Assign at least two Makers to build prototype pieces; one Stitcher to combine them; one Writer to produce all copy; one or two Collectors to gather photos, icons, and competitor screenshots; one Interviewer to write and rehearse Friday interview script; and the Sprint Master to help where needed and maintain energy.

# Choose prototype tools and build in two halves

Select tools suited to the product: linked Figma, Keynote, or PowerPoint slides for web or apps; video walkthrough or 3D-printed mockup for physical products; or role-play video or scripted interaction for services. In the morning, divide storyboard scenes among makers; in the afternoon, stitch the prototype, check it against the storyboard, rehearse the full flow, and trial it with someone outside the sprint team. Ensure it follows the storyboard exactly, looks real enough for honest reactions, takes 5-15 minutes to walk through, can be presented by the interviewer, and has completed a trial run. Thursday output is the realistic prototype, interview script, and prepared interview room.

# Choose Rumble or All-in-One

If multiple sketches win, choose either Rumble, with competing prototypes testing different approaches, or All-in-One, combining the best ideas into one prototype. Draw a simple 10-15 panel comic of the test experience from customer discovery through solution use to successful outcome; make the UI specific and include enough detail for Thursday prototype. Wednesday output is the winning solution or solutions and a detailed prototype-ready storyboard.

# Score the sprint plan and execution

Score a sprint plan or execution out of 10, awarding one point for each listed item when present and correct; report the score and missing items. The ten items require: a full-week Decider and one Sprint Master; Monday target customer and moment; hard time boxes including Crazy 8s in 8 minutes and 10am-5pm days; solo anonymous sketches without group brainstorming; Wednesday Decider Supervote rather than consensus; storyboard before prototyping; Goldilocks facade testable in 5-15 minutes with trial run; exactly five target users recruited by screener and six scheduled for no-show coverage; Friday Five-Act Interview with unexplained prototype interpretation; and debrief converting the +/-/~ grid to a next-step decision. Cap a plan at 6 if it lacks the Decider, real users, or a same-day prototype.

# Run the sticky decision

Display sketches for silent review and dot-mark interesting parts. Discuss each sketch for three minutes while the facilitator narrates and the anonymous sketcher stays silent; a scribe records standout ideas on the whiteboard. Have each person cast a non-binding vote for one solution with one sentence of rationale. The Decider receives three large dots and the Supervote decision wins.

# Use the sprint variations

The 4-day variation combines Map and Sketch on day one, then Decide, Prototype, and Test on days two through four. A remote sprint retains the schedule and uses Miro or FigJam whiteboards and Zoom. A multi-sprint sequence uses sprint one to choose broad direction, sprint two to explore the chosen solution, and sprint three to refine details.

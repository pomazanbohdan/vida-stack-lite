# Apply the monetization framework

Use this framework to design products and pricing around validated willingness to pay: validate WTP before building, diagnose the four monetization failures, segment by customer value, package features into tiers, choose a monetization model, and apply behavioral pricing.

# Price with behavioral science and communicate it

Use anchors, compromise effect, decoys and price endings to frame choice, with disciplined communication protecting price. Measure a decoy by whether it shifts choices, not whether it sells. Match charm or round endings to deal or quality position. Announce increases with delivered value before specifics and ample notice; do not apologize and discount together. Diagnose awareness, channel and packaging before changing price; price is last lever. Set day-30/60/90 triggers before launch and monitor instead of panic-discounting. Tactics must frame real value; false claims make anchors, decoys and endings deceptive.

# Avoid the listed pricing mistakes

Avoid building before pricing, cost-plus or competitor-copy pricing, direct 'would you buy this?' questions, average-WTP design, one-size offers, bundling killers, freemium as business model and panic-discounting. Instead test concept WTP, price from validated ranges, probe acceptable/expensive/prohibitive price and use forced trade-offs, segment the WTP curve, match three or four offers to WTP clusters, unbundle or cut killers, use free as acquisition capped at habit point with leader gated, and pre-set triggers while diagnosing awareness and packaging.

# Diagnose the four monetization failures

Classify struggling products by four testable failures: feature shock (features add complexity/cost without raising WTP), minivation (value priced too timidly), hidden gem (valuable byproduct lacks monetization owner), and undead (unwanted product kept alive beyond evidence). Match the cure: cut, raise, assign owner/spin out, or kill; a wrong cure worsens results. Flat WTP as features pile up signals feature shock; internal anchors or near-total wins without price pushback signal minivation; appoint owners for byproducts; set kill criteria before emotional investment. Kill products, never evidence; do not massage research to preserve a favorite.

# Choose the monetization model and price metric

Choose the price metric before price level; select a metric tracking delivered value and a model fitting customer consumption and payment. Treat freemium as conversion-oriented acquisition spend, not the pricing model or generosity program. Manage usage-price volatility and bill shock with caps, alerts or committed tiers. Account for per-seat pricing taxing collaboration and outcome pricing requiring trusted attribution. Consider a hybrid platform fee plus usage for a predictable floor and value-linked upside. A model migration reprices existing customers at once: grandfather generously and lead with value. Choose metrics customers can predict and audit; surprise bills monetize confusion, not value.

# Package features into tiers

Classify every feature as leader, filler or killer. Build good-better-best tiers around leaders, use fillers for modest value/differentiation, and unbundle or remove killers. Killer value is segment-relative, not inherently bad. Withhold the full leader from the lowest tier while leaving a taste; design the middle tier first. Target roughly 70/20/10 across middle/premium/entry; investigate weak fences if most choose bottom and possible minivation if most choose top. Bundle complementary components when they raise total WTP; unbundle when segments diverge or a killer enters. Default to three tiers, maximum four. Fence on added value, never by withholding essentials: security, privacy and data export belong in every tier.

# Research willingness to pay before building

Research WTP while the product is a concept, before specs freeze, business-case lock or coding, to learn whether customers value the idea, how much and which parts, rather than set a final price. Ask acceptable, expensive and prohibitively expensive price points; on a 1-5 purchase-probability scale count only discounted top-box 5s as positive, 4s as maybes and lower as no. Prefer feature ranking or priced-bundle trade-offs to direct questions. Frame the discussion as value research, never a quote or negotiation. Do not justify building a feature without stating its WTP range. Rebuild the living business case whenever scope, segment or price assumptions move, weekly rather than annually. Match price to delivered value, not each customer's maximum pain.

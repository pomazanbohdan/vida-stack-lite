# Run the offer diagnostic

Audit an existing offer with seven yes/no questions: does perceived value reach 10x price; is the market a starving crowd with pain, money, targetability, and growth; does the guarantee reverse risk; are there at least three named bonuses with dollar values; is there a genuine reason to act now; is the bundle difficult for a competitor to duplicate exactly; and does the name identify its audience and outcome? For each no, the source suggests adding value or revisiting Dream Outcome, switching or narrowing the market, adding risk reversal, creating objection-killing bonuses, adding truthful scarcity or a real deadline, differentiating the bundle, or renaming with MAGIC, respectively.

# Use only real scarcity and urgency

Use scarcity to limit quantity and urgency to limit time, giving people who already want the offer a reason to act. Supply limits can include genuine seats, enrollment caps, or production runs; time limits can include enrollment windows or deadline-bound bonuses. Cohort capacity is identified as the most ethical scarcity; bonus scarcity can add urgency without limiting the core offer. Evergreen urgency must be tied to real events such as onboarding cohorts or seasonal cycles. Every scarcity and urgency claim must be 100% true; never reset a countdown timer or fake a sold-out state.

# Score the offer

Score an offer against the seven Quick Diagnostic rows: assign approximately 1.4 points for each yes and round the result to a 0–10 scale, aiming for 10/10. Report the current score and the specific rows that must change to yes to reach 10. The source labels 9–10 as all or nearly all passing, including 10x perceived value, reversed risk, ethical scarcity, named bonuses with dollar values, a category-of-one bundle, and a MAGIC name; 5–6 as value and market fit but missing risk reversal, bonuses, or scarcity; and scores at or below 3 as a cost-priced commodity with no guarantee or reason to act now.

# Find a starving crowd

Choose a market with all four characteristics: massive pain, purchasing power, easy targeting, and growth. Give pain the greatest weight because people pay to stop pain faster than to gain pleasure. Easy targeting means the market is reachable through existing channels such as associations, communities, or platforms. Narrow the audience because specificity signals expertise and raises perceived value. Target genuine need and fit, never vulnerability; avoid people in crisis who cannot make rational decisions.

# Apply the Value Equation

Model perceived value as (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) divided by (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice); increase the numerator and reduce the denominator. Define the outcome from what customers want, account for time, comfort, status, and identity sacrifices, and use social proof, guarantees, or track record as evidence that may raise perceived likelihood. A guarantee can also lower perceived risk. Back every speed, effort, and results claim with data, or clearly label it aspirational instead of asserting it.

# Price on delivered value

Price according to delivered and perceived value rather than cost, aiming for a 10:1 value-to-price ratio. The source advises anchoring against the cost of not solving the problem rather than alternatives, recognizes price as a positioning signal, and says payment plans can remove price as an objection without reducing revenue. It also states that higher prices often increase conversions and attract more committed customers who invest effort and remain longer; treat these as tendencies, not guarantees.

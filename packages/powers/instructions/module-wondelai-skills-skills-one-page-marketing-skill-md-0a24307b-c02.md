# Make every channel trackable

Make every advertising dollar trackable, measurable, and intended to prompt a specific action; avoid vague brand awareness. Select channels where the target audience spends time, master one before adding another, track CAC by channel against LTV, and combine organic authority with paid speed. Each ad needs a target-focused headline, compelling offer, clear call to action, and tracking mechanism.

# Choose a niche and avatar

Select a niche using Personal fulfillment, Value to marketplace, and Profitability. Build a detailed customer avatar, focus narrowly enough that the message speaks directly to that audience, and use one niche for one marketing message and audience, not necessarily one product.

# Nurture leads with consistent value

Nurture leads who are not ready to buy through consistent value, education, and trust. Set expectations in the welcome sequence; use an approximate 3:1 value-to-ask cadence, email as the backbone with reinforcing channels, and behavior-based segmentation. Include one-click unsubscribe in every send and honor it immediately.

# Score only when every square has a criterion

Apply the Quick Diagnostic to its six corresponding squares and report that it does not provide a row for the other three. Check the referenced nine-square template and exercise for explicit criteria before deciding those squares; do not invent criteria. Report a score when the source provides enough criteria to judge all nine squares, and otherwise identify the unresolved squares and explain why the score cannot be determined.

# State a defensible unique selling proposition

Answer why a buyer should choose the business over its nearest competitor with a defensible, provable USP, not merely a slogan. Build it around specialization, a unique mechanism, a bold guarantee, a proprietary process, or an underserved niche; keep an elevator pitch under 30 seconds and apply the swap test for generic messaging.

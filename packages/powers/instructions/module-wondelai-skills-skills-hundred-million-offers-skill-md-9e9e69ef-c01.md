# Treat the offer as the primary lever

Offer is the primary business lever: a strong Grand Slam Offer can sell despite mediocre marketing; excellent marketing cannot rescue a bad offer. Fix the offer before optimizing funnels, increasing ads, or hiring salespeople.

# Stack bonuses against objections

Add bonuses to address remaining objections and specific problems or obstacles to success; assign each an independently justifiable dollar value. Present the most valuable bonus first as the anchor. Name each bonus and favor high-value, low-cost-to-deliver items such as templates, recordings, or access. Partner bonuses can add value at no cost to the seller. The source says that when stacked value exceeds the price, the core product reads as free.

# Avoid the listed offer mistakes

Avoid selling a commodity by bundling unique value into a category of one; price against Dream Outcome value rather than cost; reverse the prospect's risk with a guarantee; name each bonus and describe its value and dollar amount; use only real, verifiable scarcity; apply MAGIC instead of generic naming; and narrow an audience that is too broad.

# Reverse risk with a guarantee

Use guarantees to transfer purchase risk from buyer to seller and make acceptance psychologically safer. The five types are unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee, implied, and performance-based. The source describes unconditional full refunds without questions as simplest and strongest for low-ticket offers; conditional guarantees requiring specified steps as attracting better clients; anti-guarantees such as all sales final as suitable when demand exceeds supply; and performance-based guarantees such as meeting a metric or not paying as the strongest risk reversal. Name the guarantee and multiple guarantees may be stacked to address multiple risk types. Make claims frictionless to invoke, with no fine-print traps or hoops.

# Name the offer with MAGIC

Name an offer so that it communicates its audience, outcome, timeframe, and format clearly and briefly. MAGIC comprises Magnetic reason why (hook, event, season, or trend), Avatar, Goal (concrete Dream Outcome), time Indication, and Container word (such as challenge, blueprint, accelerator, bootcamp, system, formula, or masterclass). Use only elements that aid clarity and test 3–5 names. A name may be aspirational but must never deceptively promise an outcome customers do not actually reach.

# Build the complete offer package

Build a Grand Slam Offer as a complete package of the core offer, bonuses, guarantee, scarcity, urgency, and compelling name. Map every customer problem and obstacle between the customer and Dream Outcome to a solution and delivery vehicle. Apply Trim & Stack by removing low-value/high-cost solutions and retaining high-value/low-cost ones. Make each component nameable, independently valuable, and dollar-valued; the component values together should be at least 10 times the price. Set each component's value at what someone would actually pay for it standalone; never inflate values to fake the value-to-price gap.

# Run the ten-step offer process

For an offer built from scratch, follow the listed sequence: (1) identify and score a starving crowd on pain, purchasing power, targetability, and growth; (2) define the single most desirable Dream Outcome in the customer's words; (3) list every problem, fear, objection, and friction; (4) create a solution and delivery vehicle for each obstacle; (5) remove low-value/high-cost solutions and keep high-value/low-cost ones; (6) price at 10–20% of the Dream Outcome's value (a 10:1 to 5:1 ratio); (7) create one named bonus per remaining objection with a defensible dollar value; (8) choose a model- and risk-appropriate guarantee, name it, and make it bold; (9) use genuine limits and real deadlines for scarcity and urgency; and (10) name with MAGIC using avatar, goal, timeframe, and container, testing 3–5 variations.

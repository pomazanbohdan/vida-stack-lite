# Make useful information easy to pass along

Make useful information easy to pass along; practical value spreads as a favor that can save others time, money, or effort. Apply the source's Prospect Theory framing and Rule of 100 (percentage discounts under $100; dollar amounts over $100), recognize that niche content can be forwarded to the person who needs it, and package knowledge in lists, how-tos, and tips. Value must be genuine; do not use inflated original prices or clickbait life hacks.

# Make product use observable

Design product use to be observable so others can see it, learn from it and imitate it. Consider visible traces that remain after use, self-advertising features, or ways to make invisible usage visible. Visibility must empower users and remain under their control; keep private data, including failures, health and finances, private, and never use visibility to shame.

# Score the six STEPPS drivers

Score each of six STEPPS drivers, corresponding to the six Quick Diagnostic rows, as 0 absent, 1 present but weak, or 2 strong and deliberate, for a raw score from 0–12; map 11–12 to 10, 9–10 to 8–9, 6–8 to 6–7, 3–5 to 4–5, and 0–2 to 3-or-below. Report raw count, mapped score, and drivers to raise next to reach 10/10. The 9–10 band requires at least three strength-2 drivers, including Public or Social Currency, and a brand that passes the Trojan Horse test; 6–8 means two genuinely strong drivers but continued reliance on paid reach or product quality alone; 5-or-below means at most one weak driver and incidental sharing.

# Create social currency

Create social currency by making sharing help people appear smart, cool or informed and by giving them insider status or identity value. Use remarkable information, visible accomplishments such as leaderboards/badges/status tiers, genuine exclusivity or scarcity, or an unexpected angle on an otherwise ordinary product. Create real insider value; do not use false scarcity or manufactured exclusivity that breeds toxicity.

# Use STEPPS as a multiplier, not a checklist

Treat STEPPS as a multiplier, not a checklist: each principle can independently increase sharing, several can be activated together, and one or two done well can substantially increase word of mouth. Use the six questions to assess Social Currency (does sharing improve the sharer’s image?), Triggers (what environmental cue recalls it?), Emotion (does it evoke high-arousal feeling?), Public (can others observe use?), Practical Value (is it useful to pass along?), and Stories (is the brand embedded in a narrative?).

# Embed the brand in a retellable story

Embed the brand in a retellable narrative so the brand travels with the story like a Trojan Horse. A story fails this test if it can be retold without the brand; it must also survive casual conversation without a long setup. A funny ad that is not attributed to the brand is a failure. Stories must be true or clearly fictional; fabricated testimonials and origins damage future word of mouth.

# Link the product to frequent environmental cues

Link a product to environmental cues such as sights, sounds, times or routines so they remind people to talk about it. Prefer frequent cues over rare powerful ones, map the habitat where and when relevant contexts occur, and distinguish persistent triggers from temporary event triggers. Competitor moments may also be linked to the product. Build genuine, helpful associations and do not hijack sensitive contexts such as grief or health scares.

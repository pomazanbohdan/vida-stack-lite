# Avoid common strategy mistakes

Avoid goals masquerading as strategy, skipped diagnosis, template boilerplate, untestable fluff, refusal to choose, excessive or blue-sky objectives, evenly spread resources and treating strategy as settled truth. Replace them with a kernel, a critical challenge, explicit choices, a small set of proximate objectives, concentrated effort and evidence-based review.

# Detect the four hallmarks of bad strategy

Detect bad strategy through four hallmarks: fluff, failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, and bad strategic objectives. Test whether claims can be restated plainly, whether obstacles are named, whether goals have an actual lever, and whether objectives are feasible and choiceful. Use the negation test: statements whose opposite is absurd carry no information. Audit plans against the hallmarks and separate ambitions from mechanisms.

# Run the strategy diagnostic

Apply all eight diagnostic questions: named challenge, choiceful guiding policy, coordinated resourced actions, competitor-specific fit, proximate first objective, exploited leverage or asymmetry, explicit no-list, and prior attempt to disprove the strategy. Follow each stated repair when an answer is no.

# Explain how the ambition will be achieved

A strategy explains how an ambition will be achieved against actual obstacles through coherent action backed by honest diagnosis. It is not merely goals, vision, or wishful thinking; choose and concentrate effort and resources on one or two pivotal objectives.

# Treat strategy as a testable hypothesis

Treat strategy as a hypothesis: diagnose, formulate, test against evidence, and revise. Generate genuinely different alternatives, attack the front-runner, ask what else could explain the situation, use a virtual panel for critique rather than staged endorsement, and keep the written kernel beside an explicit no-list. Independent judgment matters when consensus conflicts with evidence.

# Build the strategy kernel

A strategy requires all three kernel elements: diagnosis of the critical challenge, a guiding policy for overcoming diagnosed obstacles, and coherent coordinated actions that carry out the policy; a document missing any element is not yet a strategy. Diagnose before prescribing; the policy should channel effort and rule out options, and actions should reinforce one another with an owner, resources, and date. A one-page kernel needs no mission/vision/values preamble. An honest diagnosis includes internal causes and should not be softened to protect egos or politics.

# Apply strength at the point of leverage

Apply strength where it has greatest effect through leverage, proximate objectives, chain-link systems, coordinated design, focus, and protected advantage. Anticipate behavior, find a pivot point, and concentrate; choose objectives close enough to hit, fix the weakest link before strengthening others, coordinate tightly when stakes are high and resources scarce, and deepen or protect advantage at the point of competition. Build isolating mechanisms on delivered value, not lock-in designed only to trap users.

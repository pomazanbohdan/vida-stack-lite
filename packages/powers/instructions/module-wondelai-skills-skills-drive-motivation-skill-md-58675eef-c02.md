# Pay enough, then use now-that rewards

Pay people enough to take money off the table, fairly and ideally above-market, then focus on autonomy, mastery, and purpose; beyond enough, more money does not increase motivation. Prefer unexpected now-that rewards given after the fact over if-then rewards promised for hitting a target, because if-then rewards create pressure and short-term thinking. When applying AMP to compensation, consult references/applications.md for worked examples and escalation tables.

# Connect work to purpose

Purpose is acting in service of something larger than oneself and provides context for autonomy and mastery; without it, autonomy is directionless and mastery hollow. Express purpose through goals, words, and policies. In products, show user impact, connect people to a larger community, and align with values. Show aggregate impact, connect individual actions to collective outcomes, and celebrate meaningful milestones over vanity metrics. When wiring impact, community, or values features, consult references/purpose.md for Goals/Words/Policies patterns and the purpose audit checklist.

# Score the motivation system against the diagnostic

Score any motivation system (product features, team incentives, gamification, or engagement loops) against the Quick Diagnostic: start at 5, add 1 for each of the first five yes answers, then subtract 2 if the sixth answer is yes because an if-then reward doing the motivating crowds out the rest. Bands: 9–10 means autonomy, mastery, and purpose are all present with no if-then crowding-out; 5–6 means one pillar carries the system and the other two are weak or extrinsic; 3 or lower means rewards, mandates, or controlling behaviors dominate and intrinsic motivation is absent. Always state the current score, failed diagnostic rows, and specific fixes to reach 10/10.

# Cultivate Type I behavior

Type X behavior is fueled by external rewards, seeks external recognition, and has short-term focus and a fixed mindset. Type I behavior is fueled by autonomy, mastery, and purpose, seeks inherent satisfaction, and has long-term focus and a growth mindset. Design products and teams to cultivate Type I behavior: it is made rather than born, does not disdain money or recognition, is renewable, and promotes well-being. When shifting a team or user base from Type X to Type I, consult references/type-i.md for the behavioral contrast and conversion tactics; consult references/case-studies.md for the named real-world AMP programs.

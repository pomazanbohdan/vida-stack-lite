# Avoid common blue-ocean mistakes

Avoid competing on the same factors, differentiation without cost focus, incrementalism, imitation and ignoring adoption. Use ERRC to create a divergent curve and pursue both cost and value changes, use the six paths to find alternatives, and plan for adoption early.

# Apply the four ERRC actions

Use the four ERRC questions: eliminate factors adding no buyer value and reduce factors below industry standard to lower cost; raise factors above standard and create new factors to increase value and attract non-customers. Test assumptions before cutting, especially for safety or accessibility factors buyers value.

# Distinguish red and blue oceans

Red oceans are existing markets where rivals compete over shrinking profits; blue oceans are new spaces where competition is irrelevant. The comparison requires creating demand and pursuing differentiation and low cost together rather than exploiting existing demand or choosing one.

# Score the strategy against the five rows

Score a strategy by how many of the five Quick Diagnostic rows it satisfies, using the source bands: 9–10 for all five rows; 7–8 when one gate is weak (four rows); 5–6 for two or three rows; ≤3 for zero or one row. Report the current score, the failed rows, and the specific ERRC/Six-Paths moves needed to reach 10/10. Do not invent a score for a row count outside these bands.

# Validate utility, price, cost and adoption in order

Validate an idea in strict sequence: exceptional buyer utility, accessible strategic price, profitable target cost, then adoption hurdles. Rework before advancing if a gate fails; set target cost from price minus target margin without sacrificing utility or deferring cost work. Identify resisting employees, partners, the public, or regulators early and address their concerns rather than steamrolling them.

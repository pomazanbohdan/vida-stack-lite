# Fix the weakest Ability Chain link

Ability is determined by the scarcest resource across Time, Money, Physical Effort, Mental Effort, Social Deviance, and Non-Routine; a sufficiently weak link breaks the behavior. Diagnose and fix the weakest link rather than the most obvious factor. Starter Steps shrink behavior to its tiniest version; defaults can reduce effort. Reduce friction only for genuinely valuable behaviors, and never make overspending, oversharing, or deletion of important data too easy without confirmation.

# Move behaviors above the Action Line

Move behaviors above the Action Line primarily by increasing Ability through removing steps, pre-filling, defaults, templates, or wizards; this is the most reliable approach. Improve prompts by anchoring them to existing routines, preferring event-based over time-based prompts, and triggering when motivation is naturally higher. Motivation increases are unreliable; needing motivation tactics may indicate the behavior is too hard.

# Avoid the listed retention mistakes

Avoid relying on motivation for retention, ignoring the weakest Ability Chain link, prompting below the Action Line, skipping celebration in onboarding, making the first action too ambitious, or copying other products without diagnosing B=MAP. Apply the corresponding fixes: make behavior tiny enough for motivation dips, audit all six factors and fix the scarcest, use event-based triggers only when motivation and ability suffice, add success states/micro-celebrations after key actions, shrink to one-field/one-action Starter Steps, and diagnose users' motivation, ability, and prompt context first.

# Use B=MAP and the Action Line

Behavior occurs when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt converge at the same moment. A prompt causes behavior only when motivation and ability are sufficient; below the Action Line prompts do not work. High motivation can compensate for low ability and vice versa, but the reliable design strategy is to make behavior easier rather than rely on increased motivation.

# Diagnose retention metrics with B=MAP

Diagnose metrics with B=MAP: low activation means first action below line—shrink onboarding to Starter Step and fix weakest Ability link; Day-1 drop-off means failed/mistimed Prompt—redesign first-day prompts and anchor to routine; Day-7 drop-off means Motivation receded and behavior is hard—reduce core difficulty; Day-30 drop-off means habit/internal Prompt absent—create tiny recipe and celebration loops; low feature adoption means feature below line for most users—friction-audit and prompt only with Motivation; notification fatigue means prompts below line—cut volume and send only with Motivation and Ability. Read case studies to see M, A, P scored independently before own diagnosis.
